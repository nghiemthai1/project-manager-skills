# Conflict and negotiation methods

Use this reference for a material negotiation, stalled dialogue or facilitated mediation. The entrypoint contains the ordinary workflow.

## Diagnose the mechanism

Start with the smallest explanation supported by evidence.

| Mechanism | Questions that discriminate it | Common repair |
|---|---|---|
| Different facts | Which source, version, cutoff or measurement differs? | Joint fact record |
| Different goals or incentives | Which result is each role accountable for? | Interest-based package or governance change |
| Scarce resource | Is availability confirmed, and what other obligation competes? | Prioritized allocation decision |
| Ambiguous role or authority | Who recommends, performs, accepts and decides this exact matter? | Decision-right clarification |
| Broken process | Which handoff, review or exception route failed? | Observable working agreement |
| Relationship rupture | Which repeated behavior and impact are documented? | Direct repair or neutral facilitation |
| Conduct or safety issue | Can participation be voluntary and free of retaliation? | Authorized policy route |

Do not choose one cause only because it is comfortable. Several mechanisms can coexist.

## Prepare an interests map

For every party, distinguish:

- **Position:** the requested outcome or stated refusal.
- **Interest:** the need, responsibility, risk or value behind the position.
- **Constraint:** a real boundary, with source and authority.
- **Contribution:** something the party or system did that affected the situation.
- **Alternative:** an executable course if no agreement is reached.
- **Reservation boundary:** the worst acceptable negotiated package before the alternative is preferable.

Ask about the other party's perspective; do not assert it as fact. In internal projects, interests often include service reliability, professional accountability, predictable workload, customer impact, compliance, decision speed and trust.

## Test alternatives and the agreement zone

An alternative must be executable without the other party's agreement. “Escalate” is not automatically a strong alternative: identify the decider, decision scope, evidence required and likely consequence. “Proceed anyway” is invalid when acceptance or authorization remains mandatory.

Compare a complete package with the best alternative. Include nonfinancial dimensions such as risk, time, reversibility, relationship, learning and future operating burden. A possible agreement zone exists only when at least one package is acceptable to all required parties and within their authority.

## Generate packages

Avoid debating one variable at a time when several variables can move. Build packages across:

- scope or phasing;
- timing and sequencing;
- resources and support;
- quality or evidence method without lowering mandatory criteria;
- risk treatment and contingency;
- review cadence and reversible trials;
- governance or escalation.

State which party carries each cost, delay or residual risk. Use objective criteria chosen before the preferred option where possible.

## Facilitate mediation

### Before the joint session

Confirm voluntary participation, scope, facilitator neutrality, confidentiality limits, decision authority and behavior expectations. Separate conversations may help the facilitator understand concerns, but clarify what may be shared. Do not use private information as hidden leverage.

### During the session

1. Read the purpose, scope and process.
2. Give each party uninterrupted time to describe events, impact and desired outcome.
3. Paraphrase and ask the speaker to correct the summary.
4. Separate agreement, disagreement and unknown evidence.
5. Reframe blame as a shared problem without erasing responsibility.
6. Identify interests and constraints.
7. Generate options without commitment.
8. Evaluate packages against criteria, alternatives and authority.
9. Read back exact terms and confirmation states.

Use a pause or separate caucus when emotion prevents listening or new confidential context changes the process. Stop and route through policy when safety, retaliation, misconduct or inability to participate fairly becomes material.

## Structure the direct conversation

A useful opening has four parts:

1. **Purpose:** the project result or working relationship to protect.
2. **Observation:** a concrete event, statement or pattern with source and date.
3. **Impact:** the effect on work, evidence, trust or decision quality.
4. **Invitation:** a question that tests the other perspective and seeks a joint next step.

Describe behavior rather than identity. State your own contribution when supported. Do not demand admission of intent as a precondition for solving the project problem.

## Test an agreement

Before closing, verify:

- exact terms and exclusions;
- who owns each action and who actually decides or accepts;
- dates or a defensible timing basis;
- evidence that demonstrates completion;
- dependencies and required resources;
- conditions, expiry or review date;
- treatment of unresolved items and dissent;
- consequence or escalation trigger if a term fails;
- affected records and prior decisions retained.

Ask each required party to read back their understanding. Record proposed or conditional terms accurately. An agreement that depends on absent authority remains a recommendation.
