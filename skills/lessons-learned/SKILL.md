# Lessons Learned

## Purpose

Capture learning another project can apply without repeating the original mistake or misapplying its remedy. Use at milestones, after experiments, or at closure. A lesson is more than a retrospective comment or an untested recommendation.

## Input

Bring outcomes, decisions, evidence, retrospective experiments, and their observed results.

Example: "Capture what Northstar learned about migration reconciliation and when that lesson applies."

If an improvement has not been tested, label it a proposed practice rather than an established lesson.

Use context already supplied. If inputs are incomplete, distinguish useful draft work from decisions that require missing evidence. Mark unknowns explicitly; never fill them with example data.

## Key Concepts

### Context makes a lesson transferable

A useful lesson explains the situation, observed outcome, mechanism or causal hypothesis, response, evidence of effect, and applicability limits. "Test earlier" contains too little information to guide another team.

### Learning versus success narrative

A failed experiment can reveal an important boundary. A successful outcome does not prove every intervention caused it. Distinguish observed association from supported causal reasoning, especially when several changes happened together.

### Adoption

A lesson creates value when a named practice, template, checklist, or decision process changes and someone checks the result. A repository of unread observations is storage, not organizational learning.

### Why this works

Context and limits prevent one project's workaround from becoming universal policy. Evidence and adoption actions make the learning reviewable and keep uncertainty visible.

## Application

1. Select a consequential outcome or decision and recover its evidence and context.
2. Separate observation, explanation, intervention, and result. Mark causal uncertainty.
3. Write the transferable principle with conditions where it applies and where it may fail.
4. State whether the practice is proposed, tested in a bounded case, adopted, or superseded. Do not claim broad validation from one project.
5. Identify a practical adoption action, owner, and review measure. Confirm ownership rather than inventing it.
6. Link the lesson to affected templates or processes and preserve its evidence.
7. Review later use, revising the lesson when new evidence changes its limits.

Use the [artifact template](template.md). Keep the deliverable concise; retain the reasoning needed to explain its consequential choices.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Slogan lesson:** "communicate better" offers no decision guidance. Explain the mechanism and changed practice.
- **Victory bias:** only successful outcomes are recorded. Capture failed responses and their boundaries.
- **Universal cure:** one project's fix becomes a mandate everywhere. State context and counterconditions.
- **Causality inflation:** a later improvement is automatically attributed to the intervention. Check alternative explanations.
- **Unowned adoption:** the lesson is published but nothing changes. Name the proposed practice change and review.
