# Project Feedback and Coaching

## Purpose

Prepare a fair project-feedback conversation and, when useful, a bounded coaching loop that improves delivery behavior or judgment. Produce observable evidence, a conversation plan, support actions, a practice assignment and review checkpoints.

Use this skill when the project leader needs to address a specific behavior or help someone develop a project skill. Use delegation and follow-through when the main gap is an unclear or unaccepted commitment. Use a retrospective for team-wide system learning, conflict resolution for competing interests or stalled dialogue, and stakeholder engagement for relationship-specific outreach without a performance or skill-development objective.

This skill does not create formal employment authority. Project leaders may give factual feedback, offer project-based practice and share necessary observations through an authorized route. Ratings, compensation, promotion, discipline, medical or personal assessment, accommodations and termination remain with the authorized line, HR or organizational process.

## Input

Works best with the shared project result, specific observed actions or omissions, dated sources, project impact, prior expectations, the contributor's relationship to the project leader, known constraints, support already offered, the desired behavior or skill and a useful review point.

Use supplied evidence before asking questions. Separate observation, impact and interpretation. Preserve sensitive employment information only when necessary and authorized; a project coaching record should not become an informal personnel file.

With partial input, draft a bounded conversation and ask only questions that change the route or intervention:

1. What specific behavior or project skill needs attention, and what source supports it?
2. What project impact occurred, and what remains interpretation or unknown?
3. Is this feedback, a coachable skill gap, an unclear commitment, a system pattern or active conflict?
4. What authority and confidentiality limits apply to this relationship?
5. What support, practice opportunity and evidence-based review are feasible?

## Key Concepts

### Route the need before designing the conversation

| Primary condition | Suitable route |
|---|---|
| Outcome, boundary, capacity or commitment was unclear | Project Delegation and Follow-Through |
| One person's observable behavior materially affected delivery | Factual project feedback |
| A specific project skill can improve through guided practice | Project coaching |
| Several people show the same process pattern | Retrospective or project-system correction |
| Parties have competing interests or dialogue has stalled | Conflict Resolution and Negotiation |
| The goal is outreach or alignment with one stakeholder | Stakeholder Engagement Advisor |
| Formal performance, conduct or protected matter exceeds project authority | Authorized line, HR, supplier or policy route |

Feedback and coaching can be combined, but they answer different questions. Feedback makes an observed gap and its consequence clear. Coaching develops the reasoning or skill needed for a better result.

### Begin with authority and privacy

| Relationship | Project leader may normally do within mandate | Another authority is normally needed for |
|---|---|---|
| Direct report | Give project feedback, coach work skills and inspect agreed results | Formal employment action under organizational policy |
| Matrixed contributor | Give factual delivery feedback and offer project practice | Allocation, formal development or performance action normally involving the line or resource manager |
| Peer or workstream lead | Discuss interface behavior and agree a project improvement | Commanding work or imposing consequences outside the accepted boundary |
| Supplier participant | Give deliverable/interface feedback and use supplier governance | Acting as supplier line management or changing commercial terms without authority |

Record only what the project needs. Share factual observations through the agreed channel when another authority must act.

### Feedback uses observable evidence

A useful conversation connects:

1. the shared project purpose or working agreement;
2. a specific observed action or omission and its source;
3. the effect on delivery, people, risk or decision quality;
4. the contributor's perspective and relevant context;
5. an observable next behavior, support and checkpoint.

Discuss behavior rather than identity. Use proportional language and correct the working diagnosis when the contributor provides contrary evidence. Feedback is a two-way fact-finding and improvement conversation, not a disguised verdict.

### Coaching needs a real practice loop

Define one project skill, current evidence, competent behavior, a bounded practice assignment, support, observation method and review date. Ask the contributor to explain their reasoning before supplying an answer. Increase autonomy when the evidence improves; revise or stop the loop when it does not address the mechanism.

Project coaching can build risk disclosure, estimation, dependency negotiation, decision framing, review preparation, technical demonstration or stakeholder facilitation. Formal career planning and promotion readiness remain with the authorized line manager.

### Trust is evidence, not a personality score

Trust grows through reliable commitments, early disclosure, competent follow-through and fair responses to bad news. Describe these observable conditions. Do not assign a trust rating based on intuition or use psychological safety to avoid clear accountability.

## Application

Use a single-shot draft when evidence is sufficient. In guided mode, ask one material question at a time, normally no more than the five input questions, then present numbered response options and a recommendation.

### 1. Establish the project and people boundary

State the shared project result, reporting relationship, actual authority, confidentiality limits and any HR, legal, safety or accommodation boundary. Stabilize urgent project exposure through the proper project process before beginning a development conversation.

### 2. Build an evidence record

Create a short chronology of the relevant expectation, observed action or omission, warnings, support and project results. Mark interpretations and causal explanations as supported, contradicted or unknown. Check whether capacity, inputs or decision rights better explain the result.

### 3. Select the response

Present the relevant numbered options:

1. factual feedback and a concrete next behavior;
2. coaching with a bounded practice loop;
3. feedback followed by coaching when both clarity and capability matter;
4. obstacle removal, renewed delegation, retrospective, conflict resolution or stakeholder engagement when another mechanism owns the problem;
5. line, resource or supplier escalation when formal authority or repeated material consequences exceed the project role.

Recommend the smallest response that addresses the evidence and protects the project. Do not use open-ended coaching to delay a necessary authority decision.

### 4. Prepare the artifacts

Use the [template](template.md) to produce applicable parts:

- **Feedback conversation:** purpose, observation, impact, uncertainty, questions, requested behavior and close.
- **Coaching plan:** project skill, current evidence, practice, support, observation and review.
- **Support actions:** obstacle, action, owner, timing and success evidence.
- **Review checkpoints:** behavior or result evidence and the rule to recognize, adjust, stop or escalate.

A draft conversation is not sent or scheduled without authorization. Suggested assignments remain proposed until accepted.

### 5. Hold the conversation and confirm the loop

Invite the contributor's account before settling the diagnosis. Correct the evidence record when warranted. Confirm the requested project behavior, support, practice boundary, review evidence and what remains with another authority.

For a matrixed contributor, involve the resource or line manager when allocation or formal development decisions are required. For a supplier participant, route formal management through the supplier lead and contract governance.

### 6. Review and close or change the response

Inspect the agreed evidence. Recognize specific improvement. If conditions changed, revise the plan prospectively. If the practice does not improve the target skill, reassess the mechanism. If a supported intervention does not change a recurring material result, protect the project and escalate the factual chronology through the correct authority.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Personality feedback:** “unreliable” or “not strategic” replaces an observed action and consequence. Use dated evidence and a concrete next behavior.
- **Feedback before diagnosis:** a missing environment or conflicting priority is treated as attitude. Check the work system first.
- **Vague coaching:** “communicate better” has no skill, practice or observation method. Define the project behavior and real assignment.
- **Advice disguised as coaching:** the leader supplies every answer. Ask for reasoning and teach only the missing part.
- **Coaching forever:** repeated material misses receive another open-ended cycle. Use a review date and escalation trigger.
- **Shadow line management:** project notes contain ratings, threats or personal diagnoses. Keep formal people action with authorized management.
- **Private detail in project records:** sensitive context is copied into status artifacts. Record only the necessary project consequence and action.
- **Conversation as completion:** the meeting occurs but behavior is never reviewed. Inspect agreed evidence and close, revise or escalate.
