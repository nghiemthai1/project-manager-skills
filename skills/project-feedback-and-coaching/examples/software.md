# Relay: improve early risk disclosure

Fictional training scenario, 16 October 2026. Omar coordinates technical work for the B1 pilot. Two integration updates arrived after the dependency review, and the latest update was the first record to show that the recovery environment was unavailable. Mina is the project manager. Omar's line manager, formal performance process and accepted allocation are not supplied.

## Evidence and route

| Signal | Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| Late updates | Two promised updates arrived after the dependency review | Observable project behavior |
| Late blocker visibility | Recovery-environment issue first appeared in the latest update | Decisions used an incomplete picture |
| Early-warning expectation | Exact behavior was not recorded | Leader contribution and clarity gap |
| Capacity and priority | Not supplied | Must remain unknown |
| Motivation or reliability | No direct evidence | Do not diagnose character |

Recommended sequence:

1. Mark environment and timing uncertain in project controls.
2. Give factual feedback and invite Omar's chronology.
3. Confirm the early-warning behavior and remove reporting obstacles.
4. Use two real control cycles to practice risk-disclosure judgment.
5. Change route if allocation, inputs or repeated material consequences remain.

Mina may give project feedback and coach project work. Nothing supplied authorizes Mina to rate Omar formally or impose an employment consequence.

## Feedback conversation

“I want technical risk visible early enough for the team to act. The last two integration updates arrived after the dependency review, and the latest was the first time the unavailable recovery environment appeared in that record. Our schedule and readiness discussions therefore used an incomplete picture. I may not have made the early-warning expectation or reporting burden clear enough. Can you walk me through what you knew, when, and what prevented an earlier signal?”

Questions:

- When did the environment issue become material to the recovery path?
- Which update fields or evidence were unclear?
- Which priorities, inputs or dependencies affected disclosure?
- What lightweight signal could reach Mina before the full update?
- What support or authority is missing?

Requested behavior: when a blocker could change dependency, readiness or date decisions, disclose the evidence, uncertainty and affected decision when known. The exact cutoff and feasible reporting load must be confirmed rather than assumed.

## Coaching plan

**Project skill:** early risk and dependency disclosure.

**Competent behavior:** identify the earliest material signal, distinguish fact from forecast, name the affected decision and surface it through the agreed route.

**Practice:** for two control cycles, Omar prepares the current update and explains which evidence became material first. Mina asks for his reasoning before suggesting changes. The dependency receiver checks whether the signal was usable.

**Support:** one reporting format, clear review cutoff, confirmed environment ownership and allocation decision where needed.

**Review evidence:** signal timing, decision usefulness and completeness. Message count is not a success measure.

## Checkpoints

| Checkpoint | Evidence | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Dry run | One update with explicit facts, unknowns and affected decisions | Clarify the model and remove friction |
| Next dependency review | Material signal arrives before the accepted cutoff | Recognize improvement or adjust support |
| Following control cycle | Behavior repeats without close prompting | Close coaching and retain normal review |
| Material late signal after clear expectation and support | Factual chronology and project impact | Protect delivery and involve the actual resource or line authority |

**Repair:** “Omar is unreliable and needs a warning” is unsupported. The corrected intervention names the observed behavior, the leader's possible contribution, the practice and the authority boundary.
