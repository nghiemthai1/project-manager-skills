# Project Delegation and Follow-Through

## Purpose

Create or repair a project commitment that a contributor can actually carry. Produce a delegation brief, confirmation record, support actions and review checkpoints with an explicit escalation boundary.

Use this skill after roles are known but the outcome, authority, capacity, evidence or acceptance remains unclear. A RACI cell names a relationship to work; it does not prove that the person accepted the result, timing or resources. A capacity plan tests feasibility; it does not create the commitment.

This skill governs project work, not employment status. A project leader may clarify outcomes, coordinate support, inspect agreed evidence and escalate project consequences. Formal ratings, compensation, promotion, discipline, accommodations and termination remain with the authorized line, HR or organizational process.

## Input

Works best with the desired outcome, included and excluded work, success evidence, recipient and reporting relationship, project mandate, required decisions, capacity and dependencies, requested timing, prior confirmations, support already provided and any missed-commitment history.

Use supplied facts before asking questions. Preserve exact dates and identifiers. Separate proposed assignments from accepted commitments, and observed results from explanations about motive.

With partial input, produce a bounded draft and ask only questions that change the commitment or response:

1. What result is being delegated, and what evidence will show it is usable?
2. What authority does the recipient have, and which decisions remain reserved?
3. Has the recipient accepted the outcome, capacity and timing? What record shows that?
4. Which constraints or dependencies could make the commitment infeasible?
5. What happened at prior checkpoints, and what decision is needed if the next one misses?

## Key Concepts

### Match the delegation to the relationship

| Relationship | Project leader may normally do within mandate | Another authority is normally needed for |
|---|---|---|
| Direct report | Delegate project work, supply resources, inspect evidence and adjust project autonomy | Formal employment action under organizational policy |
| Matrixed contributor | Negotiate a project commitment, clarify interfaces, support work and record consequences | Reprioritizing other obligations or changing formal allocation |
| Peer or workstream lead | Agree outcomes, interfaces, evidence and escalation paths | Commanding work outside the accepted project boundary |
| Supplier participant | Use contract deliverables, acceptance and supplier governance | Acting as supplier line management or changing commercial terms without authority |

Record the actual mandate. A project-manager title alone does not create authority over people, budgets, acceptance or supplier staffing.

### Assignment, delegation and acceptance differ

An assignment names who is expected to relate to the work. A delegation defines the result, boundaries, decisions and support. An accepted commitment adds the recipient's confirmation that the outcome, authority, capacity and timing are feasible.

Silence, meeting attendance, a RACI cell or a name in a tracker is not acceptance. Record the read-back or mark the commitment proposed.

### Diagnose the commitment mechanism

Test the conditions before selecting a response:

- **Clarity:** the outcome, boundary, priority and success evidence were understood.
- **Authority:** the recipient could make the expected decisions.
- **Capacity and capability:** the required time and skill were available or supportable.
- **Inputs:** dependencies, access, tools and information were usable.
- **Acceptance:** the recipient confirmed the commitment and disclosed later changes.
- **Support:** obstacles and course corrections received a timely response.

A missed result does not identify its cause. When several people fail in the same way, inspect the project system and priorities before individualizing the problem.

### Checkpoints should change with risk and readiness

Use closer review for novel, irreversible, regulated or highly coupled work and for unproven project-specific readiness. Use wider autonomy when evidence is strong and work is reversible. Each temporary control needs an exit condition that restores autonomy or changes the route.

Inspect decisions, interfaces and evidence rather than prescribing every action. A checkpoint without a decision rule is only a meeting.

### Escalate the remaining authority gap

Recurring misses require a chronology: assigned result, acceptance state, changed conditions, warnings, support, observed impact and checkpoint results. Escalate the exact allocation, priority, supplier or people decision beyond project authority. Do not convert an unsupported character judgment into a project record.

## Application

Use a single-shot draft when context is sufficient. In guided mode, ask one material question at a time, normally no more than the five input questions, then present numbered response options and a recommendation.

### 1. Establish the result and authority boundary

State the required project outcome, success evidence, actual acceptor, recipient relationship and project leader's mandate. Identify reserved decisions and confidentiality limits. Protect an urgent project exposure within existing authority without pre-judging the person.

### 2. Build the commitment chronology

Record what was requested, proposed, accepted, changed, disclosed and delivered. Compare the commitment with capacity, inputs and decision rights. Mark each explanation supported, contradicted or unknown.

### 3. Select the response

Offer the relevant numbered options:

1. create or renew the delegation with clearer boundaries;
2. remove an obstacle or obtain an allocation, priority or dependency decision;
3. use [Project Feedback and Coaching](../project-feedback-and-coaching/SKILL.md) for an observable behavior or project-skill gap;
4. use conflict resolution when parties have competing interests or dialogue has stalled;
5. escalate a repeated material gap through the actual line, resource or supplier authority.

Recommend the least severe response that addresses the evidenced mechanism and still protects the project.

### 4. Prepare the delegation record

Use the [template](template.md) to capture:

- purpose, outcome, boundaries and success evidence;
- decision rights and reserved decisions;
- confirmed resources, capacity and dependencies;
- recipient, timing, read-back and acceptance state;
- support actions, checkpoints and early-warning rules;
- autonomy level, exit condition and escalation trigger.

Suggested owners and dates remain proposed until accepted. Drafting a delegation does not assign another manager's staff or alter a supplier contract.

### 5. Confirm the commitment

Invite the recipient to explain the result, constraints and decisions in their own words. Resolve mismatches. Record what the project leader will provide, what the recipient accepts, what remains with another authority and how changes will be disclosed.

### 6. Review evidence and adjust

At each checkpoint compare the agreed evidence with the observed result. Recognize reliable follow-through, remove controls when the exit condition is met, or change the response when conditions changed. If clarity and support do not improve a material repeated gap, escalate the factual record and precise decision needed.

Preserve the chronology. A revised commitment updates the plan prospectively; it does not erase the prior result.

## Common Pitfalls

- **RACI as acceptance:** a responsibility cell is treated as an accepted result and date. Obtain a read-back and record feasibility.
- **Task list without an outcome:** detailed instructions hide unclear success evidence. Delegate the usable result and decision boundary.
- **Capacity by assumption:** a matrixed contributor is named without confirmed allocation. Obtain the resource decision or mark the date proposed.
- **Micromanagement without an exit:** temporary close review becomes permanent. Define evidence that restores autonomy.
- **Character diagnosis:** “unreliable” replaces a chronology. Record actions, conditions and project consequences.
- **Endless recommitment:** every miss receives a new date with the same mechanism. Change the constraint or escalate the remaining authority gap.
- **Checkpoint theatre:** meetings occur without evidence or a decision rule. Inspect the result and act on the agreed trigger.
