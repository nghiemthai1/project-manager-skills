# Delegation and follow-through methods

Use this reference for a material delegation, an unaccepted assignment or a recurring missed commitment.

## Diagnose before changing the commitment

Test the project mechanism in this order:

1. **Outcome:** Was the required result and reason clear?
2. **Boundary:** Were included work, exclusions and acceptance explicit?
3. **Acceptance:** Did the recipient confirm ownership and timing?
4. **Authority:** Could the recipient make the expected decisions?
5. **Capacity:** Was availability accepted after other obligations and support work?
6. **Inputs:** Were dependencies, access, tools and information usable?
7. **Capability:** Did the person have or receive the needed skill and context?
8. **Support:** Did the project leader identify drift and respond in time?
9. **Repeated result:** After these conditions were addressed, did the agreed result still fail?

Record evidence for each condition. A missed date does not prove motive. Similar misses across several people point toward priorities, interfaces, process or leadership conditions that need inspection.

## Build the commitment

A usable delegation defines:

- why the outcome matters;
- the result and acceptance evidence;
- included and excluded work;
- decisions the recipient may make and decisions reserved;
- constraints, risks and assumptions;
- confirmed capacity, tools, information and dependencies;
- interfaces and affected stakeholders;
- checkpoints sized to risk and readiness;
- early-warning and escalation triggers;
- the read-back that confirms acceptance.

Do not hide an unresolved acceptance authority inside the delegation. The work owner, project coordinator and acceptor may be different people.

## Match review to readiness and risk

Use closer review when the work is novel, irreversible, regulated, highly coupled or performed without demonstrated readiness. Use wider autonomy when relevant evidence is strong and the work is reversible.

State what evidence will reduce or increase review. Closer review should inspect consequential reasoning, interfaces and evidence rather than prescribe every action.

## Respond to recurring missed commitments

| Condition | Project response |
|---|---|
| First material miss with unclear delegation | Clarify and re-contract the work |
| Confirmed obstacle or allocation conflict | Remove the obstacle or obtain an authority decision |
| Observable behavior affected work and was not surfaced | Route to factual project feedback and agree an early-warning behavior |
| Specific project-skill gap | Route to a bounded coaching and practice loop |
| Repeated miss after clarity, support and accepted checkpoints | Protect the project and involve the actual line, resource or supplier authority |
| Conduct, safety, retaliation or protected matter | Use the applicable authorized policy route immediately |

Escalation records project facts, support provided, current consequence and the decision needed. It should not contain amateur diagnoses or punitive recommendations outside project authority.

## Review fairly

At each checkpoint:

1. compare agreed evidence with the observed result;
2. distinguish improvement, unchanged behavior and changed conditions;
3. recognize specific progress;
4. adjust support or autonomy;
5. update the project plan prospectively;
6. escalate only the remaining decision or authority gap.

Support does not erase prior impact, and prior failure does not justify ignoring current improvement.
