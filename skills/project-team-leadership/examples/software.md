# Relay: lead earlier risk disclosure without assuming motive

Fictional training scenario, 16 October 2026. Omar coordinates the technical work for the B1 pilot. Two promised integration updates arrived after the dependent review window, and the latest update revealed that the recovery environment was still unavailable. Mina is the project manager. Omar's line manager, formal performance process and accepted allocation are not supplied.

## Evidence and diagnosis

| Commitment or condition | Evidence | Result | Diagnosis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical update before dependency review | Two updates arrived after the review window | Dependent planning used stale assumptions | Observable project behavior |
| Recovery environment | Still unavailable when the latest update arrived | Required evidence path remains uncertain | Input/system obstacle |
| Omar's motivation or reliability | No direct evidence | Unknown | Do not diagnose character |
| Capacity and priority | Not supplied | Unknown | Resource/line confirmation needed |
| Update expectation | Timing was promised, but exact early-warning behavior is not recorded | Partial clarity | Re-contract disclosure expectations |

Recommended sequence:

1. Protect the project by marking environment and timing uncertain.
2. Hold a factual feedback and re-delegation conversation.
3. Confirm capacity, priority and input ownership.
4. Coach a bounded early-risk-disclosure practice.
5. Involve the resource or line manager if allocation or repeated nonperformance remains after clarity and support.

Mina may coordinate technical delivery and give project feedback. Nothing in the scenario authorizes Mina to issue a formal performance rating or employment consequence.

## Delegation brief

**Purpose:** provide current technical evidence early enough for dependency, readiness and date decisions.

**Outcome:** a weekly technical control update that identifies completed evidence, blocked inputs, forecast changes and decisions needed. A material blocker or forecast change is disclosed when known rather than held for the weekly update.

**Boundary:** Omar coordinates the technical content within his accepted workstream role. Mina owns integrated project reporting. Lena retains security acceptance. Ada retains the applicable mandate decisions.

**Success evidence:** update delivered before the agreed review cutoff; every material blocker has source, owner or ownership gap, impact, next action and disclosure date. Exact cutoff and accepted capacity must be confirmed.

**Decision rights:** Omar may explain technical options and recommend sequence. He does not approve security evidence, scope or date changes. Mina may escalate integrated consequences but does not assign unknown capacity.

**Checkpoints:** proposed first dry run, next dependency review and the following control cycle. Dates remain unconfirmed until calendar and allocation are accepted.

**Return trigger:** another material blocker is disclosed after the decision window, or confirmed workload makes the cadence infeasible.

## Feedback conversation

Opening:

“I want us to make technical risk visible early enough for the team to act. The last two integration updates arrived after the dependency review, and the latest was the first time the unavailable recovery environment appeared in that record. The result is that our schedule and readiness conversations used an incomplete picture. I may not have made the early-warning expectation or update burden clear enough. Can you walk me through what you knew, when, and what prevented an earlier signal?”

Questions:

- Which update fields or evidence were unclear?
- When did the environment issue become material to the recovery path?
- Which priorities or dependencies affected the update?
- What is a feasible early-warning behavior before the next review?
- What support or authority is missing?

Requested result: agree a lightweight disclosure rule and a feasible update commitment, with capacity and environment ownership confirmed. Close with a read-back; do not describe the conversation as accepted until Omar confirms.

## Support actions

| Need | Action | Owner / authority | Success evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recovery environment | Identify the actual provisioning owner and usable date or escalate the ownership gap | Mina coordinates; performer/authority unknown | Named accepted owner and usable condition |
| Update burden | Reduce duplicate reporting and provide one project control format | Mina | One source used at the review |
| Capacity / priority | Confirm Omar's accepted allocation and competing commitments | Resource or line manager with Omar | Explicit allocation or priority decision |
| Acceptance clarity | Confirm the recovery evidence expected | Lena | Applicable criterion and review lead time |

## Project-development plan

Skill: early risk and dependency disclosure.

Practice: for two control cycles, Omar prepares the update from current evidence, identifies the earliest material signal and explains the decision consequence. Mina asks for the reasoning before suggesting changes. Lena or the dependency receiver verifies whether the evidence is usable.

Review: after two cycles or sooner if a material signal is late. Evidence is timeliness, completeness and receiver usability, not the number of messages.

Formal development ownership remains unknown; Mina may share factual project observations with the authorized line manager through the agreed channel.

## Review checkpoints

| Checkpoint | Evidence | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Dry run | Complete update with explicit unknowns | Clarify format and support |
| Next dependency review | Update and material signals arrive before the accepted cutoff | Recognize improvement or adjust obstacles |
| Following control cycle | Behavior repeats without close prompting | Restore wider autonomy |
| Any repeated late material disclosure after support | Chronology, impact and accepted expectations | Protect project and involve actual resource/line authority |

**Repair:** “Omar is unreliable and needs a warning” is unsupported and outside Mina's supplied authority. The corrected plan addresses the project evidence, support and escalation boundary.
