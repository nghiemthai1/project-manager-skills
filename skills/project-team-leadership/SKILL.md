# Project Team Leadership

## Purpose

Lead sustained project work through clear delegation, timely feedback, practical coaching, obstacle removal and accountable follow-up. Produce a usable delegation brief, feedback conversation, support actions, project-development plan and review checkpoints.

Use this skill when RACI names roles and capacity planning shows availability, but delivery still depends on how people exercise judgment, surface problems, learn and keep commitments. It applies to direct reports, matrixed contributors, workstream leads and supplier participants, with different authority in each relationship.

This skill does not turn a project manager into a line manager. Project leadership can clarify project outcomes, coordinate work, give factual delivery feedback, offer support and escalate project consequences. Formal performance ratings, compensation, promotion, discipline, medical or personal assessment, employment accommodations and termination remain with the authorized line, HR or organizational process.

## Input

Works best with the project outcome, team structure, reporting relationships, the project manager's mandate, delegated work, observed commitments and results, capacity and skill evidence, support already offered, prior conversations and the desired change.

Use source facts before asking questions. Separate an observed result from a judgment about character or motivation. Preserve sensitive employment information only when necessary and authorized; a project action record should not become an informal personnel file.

With partial input, produce a bounded draft and ask only questions that change the response:

1. What specific outcome, commitment or behavior needs attention, and what evidence shows the gap?
2. Is this person a direct report, matrixed contributor, peer, workstream lead or supplier participant?
3. What project authority does the user actually hold over work, priorities, feedback and resources?
4. Which causes are evidenced: unclear outcome, missing skill, unavailable capacity, dependency, weak support, conflicting incentive or repeated choice?
5. What support and feedback have already occurred, and what result or review date is needed?

## Key Concepts

### Start with the authority map

Authority depends on the relationship and the decision.

| Role relationship | Project leader may usually do within mandate | Requires confirmation or another authority |
|---|---|---|
| Direct report | Delegate project work, give feedback, coach and inspect agreed results | Formal rating, compensation, promotion, discipline and employment action follow policy |
| Matrixed contributor | Clarify project need, negotiate commitments, support work and give factual feedback | Reprioritizing other obligations or formal performance action normally involves the resource/line manager |
| Peer or workstream lead | Agree interfaces, evidence and escalation paths | Commanding work outside the agreed project boundary |
| Supplier participant | Apply the contract, deliverable, acceptance and governance route | Acting as the supplier's line manager or changing commercial terms without authority |

Record actual delegation rather than assuming it from the project-manager title.

### Diagnose the delivery mechanism

A missed result does not reveal its cause. Test:

- **Clarity:** outcome, boundary, acceptance and priority were understood.
- **Capability:** the required knowledge or skill exists or can be learned in time.
- **Capacity:** accepted availability covers the work and support burden.
- **Inputs and system:** dependencies, access, tools and process enable the result.
- **Decision rights:** the person can make the decisions expected of them.
- **Support and feedback:** blockers and course corrections arrive in time.
- **Commitment:** the person accepted the commitment and surfaced changes promptly.

Motivation, intent and attitude are hypotheses until evidenced. If several people fail in the same way, inspect the system before labeling individuals.

### Choose the leadership response

1. **Delegate or re-clarify** when the outcome, boundary, decision rights or success evidence are weak.
2. **Remove obstacles or rebalance** when capacity, inputs, access or priority conflicts prevent performance.
3. **Give project feedback** when observable behavior has a material impact and the person needs a clear opportunity to respond.
4. **Coach and practice** when the person can grow a specific project skill through guided repetition and feedback.
5. **Escalate through line, resource or supplier governance** when formal authority, allocation, repeated nonperformance or employment action exceeds the project role.

These responses can form a sequence. Do not hide a recurring project consequence behind endless coaching, and do not escalate a solvable clarity problem as misconduct.

### Delegate outcomes, boundaries and decisions

A delegation brief contains purpose, outcome, included and excluded work, success evidence, decision rights, constraints, resources, dependencies, reporting expectations, checkpoints and escalation triggers. Match autonomy to task risk and demonstrated project-specific readiness. Temporary closer review must have an exit condition; otherwise it becomes permanent micromanagement.

Delegation is accepted only when the recipient confirms the outcome, authority, capacity and timing. A name in RACI is not proof of an accepted commitment.

### Feedback uses observable evidence

Prepare the conversation around:

1. the shared project purpose;
2. a specific observed action or omission and its source;
3. the effect on delivery, people, risk or trust;
4. the recipient's perspective and relevant context;
5. a concrete next behavior, support and checkpoint.

Discuss behavior rather than identity. Name uncertainty and the leader's own contribution where supported. Feedback is a two-way fact-finding and improvement conversation, not a disguised verdict.

### Coaching needs a project skill and a practice loop

Define the skill to improve, current evidence, a realistic practice assignment, available support, observation method and review date. Ask questions that develop judgment before giving the answer. Formal career development belongs with the authorized line manager; the project leader may propose project-based practice and share factual observations through the agreed channel.

Trust grows through repeated reliable commitments, early disclosure, competent follow-through and fair responses to bad news. Do not turn trust into an unsupported personality score.

## Application

Use a single-shot draft when enough context is supplied. In guided mode, ask one material question at a time and normally stop after the five input questions. Then show numbered response options and recommend a sequence.

### 1. Establish the project and people boundary

Identify the required project result, actual reporting relationship, delegated authority, confidentiality limits and any HR, legal, safety or accommodation boundary. If urgent stabilization is needed, protect the project first without pre-judging the person.

### 2. Build an evidence-based diagnosis

Create a short chronology of commitments, changes, warnings, support and results. Compare the assigned outcome with actual capacity, inputs and decision rights. Mark each causal explanation as supported, contradicted or unknown.

For repeated missed commitments, inspect whether dates were accepted, whether changes were disclosed early, whether the same obstacle recurs and whether previous support changed the result.

### 3. Select the response

Present relevant numbered options:

- renewed delegation with clearer boundaries;
- obstacle removal or resource/priority negotiation;
- direct project feedback and a recovery commitment;
- coaching with a bounded practice loop;
- line/resource/supplier escalation with a factual project record.

Explain the benefit, authority requirement and risk of each. Recommend the least severe response that addresses the evidenced mechanism and protects the project.

For deeper response selection, use [the leadership methods](references/project-team-leadership-methods.md).

### 4. Prepare the leadership artifacts

Use the [template](template.md) to produce all applicable parts:

- **Delegation brief:** outcome, boundary, decision rights, resources, checkpoints and acceptance state.
- **Feedback conversation:** opening, observations, impact, questions, requested behavior and close.
- **Support actions:** obstacle, action, owner, timing and success evidence.
- **Project-development plan:** skill, practice, support, observation and review.
- **Review checkpoints:** evidence, reviewer, timing and action if the result misses.

Suggested assignments remain proposals until accepted. A draft conversation is not sent or scheduled without authorization.

### 5. Hold the conversation and confirm commitments

Invite the person's account before settling the diagnosis. Correct the record when new evidence changes it. Confirm what the project leader will do, what the contributor accepts, what remains with another authority and how changes will be disclosed.

For a matrixed contributor, involve the resource or line manager when priority, allocation or formal development decisions are required. Share only the necessary factual project information.

### 6. Review results and adjust

Inspect the agreed evidence at the checkpoint. Recognize improvement specifically. If a supported intervention does not change the result, reassess the mechanism and project exposure. Escalate recurring material gaps through the correct authority with the chronology, support provided, current impact and exact decision needed.

Do not retroactively rewrite missed commitments after a supportive conversation. Preserve history and update the current plan prospectively.

## Common Pitfalls

- **RACI as delegation:** a responsibility cell is treated as an accepted outcome, capacity and date. Use a delegation read-back with decision rights and evidence.
- **Character diagnosis:** “unreliable” replaces a chronology of missed updates and their conditions. Describe behavior and test the mechanism.
- **Project manager as shadow line manager:** informal ratings or threats appear in project notes. Keep formal people action with authorized management and policy.
- **Feedback without support:** the person is told to improve while a missing environment or competing priority remains. Remove or explicitly govern the obstacle.
- **Coaching forever:** repeated material misses receive another open-ended coaching cycle. Use bounded practice, evidence and an escalation trigger.
- **Micromanagement without exit:** temporary close review becomes permanent task instruction. Define what evidence restores autonomy.
- **Private employment detail in project records:** sensitive context is distributed with status notes. Record only the project consequence and authorized action.
- **Checkpoint theatre:** meetings occur, but no observable behavior or delivery evidence changes. Review results, not attendance.
