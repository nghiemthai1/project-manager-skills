# Northstar: a migration with evidence gates

This fictional walkthrough follows the [shared scenario](../SCENARIOS.md). It demonstrates a hybrid project with controlled commitments and iterative learning. Additional closure exhibits are labeled below; they are not inferred from a forecast.

## 1. Authorize the right boundary

Use [Project Charter](../../skills/project-charter/SKILL.md), [Stakeholder Map](../../skills/stakeholder-map/SKILL.md), and [Delivery Approach Advisor](../../skills/delivery-approach-advisor/SKILL.md).

Scope includes active tickets, agreed attachments, access mapping, reconciliation, support training, archive access, and recoverable cutover. Historical analytics redesign is excluded. Noel sponsors the project, Jules coordinates, Chen leads migration, Beck is vendor contact, Saira accepts business results, and Rosa accepts operations handover.

M-D001 on 5 October approves B1: cutover 21 November and USD 240,000 performance budget. USD 24,000 management reserve is held separately. Rehearsals provide feedback about uncertain data while the cutover remains subject to explicit governance.

## 2. Define acceptance before counting progress

Use [Scope and Work Breakdown](../../skills/scope-and-wbs/SKILL.md), [Acceptance and Traceability](../../skills/acceptance-and-traceability/SKILL.md), and [Integrated Project Planning](../../skills/integrated-project-planning/SKILL.md).

| Requirement | Verification | Acceptance responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| Agreed population migrated | Population reconciliation and exception treatment | Saira |
| Attachment relationships preserved | Identity/linkage checks against agreed rules | Saira, with Chen supplying evidence |
| Service recoverable | Applicable restore demonstration | Readiness authority with Rosa's service conditions |
| Support ready | Training, access, runbooks, and accepted coverage | Rosa |
| Archive access available | Agreed retrieval workflow demonstrated | Business/service owners as assigned |

A test plan is not an execution result. Equal file counts are useful population evidence but do not prove correct relationships. Historical analytics does not enter scope merely because a stakeholder calls it a small request.

## 3. Plan the uncertain work

Use [Estimation Advisor](../../skills/estimation-advisor/SKILL.md), [Milestone Schedule](../../skills/milestone-schedule/SKILL.md), and [Resource Capacity Plan](../../skills/resource-capacity-plan/SKILL.md).

The earlier attachment-free migration is not a sound analogy for the entire new effort. Use comparable setup evidence where relevant and a representative rehearsal for attachment uncertainty. Distinguish fixed setup, volume-dependent work, exceptions, and restore.

An instructional rehearsal network has A=2 working days, B=3 after A, C=3 after A, D=2 after B and C, and E=0 after D. Both branches are critical at seven working days. If Chen is required full-time on B and C, parallel execution is infeasible; resource sequencing must be resolved outside the simple CPM helper.

A separate weekly capacity subcase gives Chen 28 available hours and 32 demanded, a four-hour overload. Vendor capacity remains unknown until confirmed. Spare headcount on paper does not resolve skill or timing constraints.

## 4. Review the 23 October evidence

Use [Project Budget](../../skills/project-budget/SKILL.md), [Dependency Map](../../skills/dependency-map/SKILL.md), and [Status Report](../../skills/status-report/SKILL.md).

PV=USD 80,000, EV=60,000, AC=75,000, BAC=240,000. CV=-15,000, SV=-20,000, CPI=0.8, and SPI=0.75. A CPI-based EAC is USD 300,000. The forecast exceeds BAC by USD 60,000 and the original USD 264,000 total envelope by USD 36,000.

M-DEP01 is vendor mapping expected 4 November and needed 2 November, giving -2 calendar days of local margin. Neither SPI nor that local gap proves a particular calendar delay for cutover. The remaining schedule and readiness evidence are needed.

The escalation to Noel compares feasible phasing, timing, scope, and funding options without treating reserve as automatically released or a forecast as spending authority.

## 5. Preserve the risk-to-issue transition

Use [RAID Log](../../skills/raid-log/SKILL.md), [Meeting Knowledge Graph](../../skills/meeting-knowledge-graph/SKILL.md), and [Change Request](../../skills/change-request/SKILL.md).

M-R01 described possible attachment identifier problems. On 6 November, a rehearsal reproduces missing links. Create M-I002 and retain the risk's earlier history. A vendor investigation is not a completed repair, and a mentioned Monday deadline is not a commitment when the speaker explicitly declines it.

M-CR02 on 9 November authorizes phased migration, 28 November cutover, B2 performance budget USD 300,000, and funding. The bridge is original performance budget USD 240,000, the original USD 24,000 reserve explicitly reallocated/released into the revised authorization, and USD 36,000 additional funding. The original baseline remains in the history.

## 6. Make the cutover decision from current evidence

Use [Release Readiness](../../skills/release-readiness/SKILL.md) and [Release and Handover](../../skills/release-and-handover/SKILL.md).

| Date | Event | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 25 November | Restore demonstration fails | Hold readiness despite an approved revised plan |
| 27 November | Rehearsal succeeds and Saira accepts applicable results | Evidence supports reassessment; no invented universal waiver |
| 28 November | Cutover under Noel's go decision | Authorized execution with explicit interim service coverage |
| 2 December | Rosa accepts operations handover | Defined responsibilities transfer to the service owner |
| 4 December | Project closure approved | Separate closure decision, supported by its own packet |

A go decision permits the defined execution; it does not prove the commands succeeded. Retain actual reconciliation, monitoring, and recovery records. Full handover may follow cutover, but the interim service owner must never be implicit.

## 7. Close with financial and residual evidence

Use [Retrospective](../../skills/retrospective/SKILL.md), [Lessons Learned](../../skills/lessons-learned/SKILL.md), and [Project Closure](../../skills/project-closure/SKILL.md).

The lesson is that reconciliation must cover meaningful relationships as well as counts. The new checks remain proposed until their behavior is tested, including whether they detect a known bad linkage. Their appropriate scope depends on the migration; no universal sample size is invented.

Additional fictional closure exhibits for this complete walkthrough:

- M-F001, 3 December: final actual cost USD 296,000, no remaining project invoices or accruals, and finance accepts the reconciliation. This is not inferred from B2.
- M-H001, 2 December: Rosa accepts runbooks, access, support coverage, archive operations, and the known-issue disposition. Remaining ordinary service improvements have explicit accepted service ownership.
- M-C001, 4 December: Noel approves closure after the acceptance, financial, and residual-obligation review. Final actual cost is USD 56,000 above B1 and USD 4,000 below B2.
- M-BR001: Saira accepts the 15 January 2027 benefit review, using observed service-desk workflow and archive-access outcomes. No future benefit is claimed as already realized.

The closure skill's shorter example omits these exhibits and correctly reports financial evidence unavailable. A full closure packet becomes supportable only when the additional records are supplied.

## Transfer the reasoning

Preserve the distinction between uncertainty and observed failure, forecast and authorization, technical delivery and acceptance, and release and handover. Those distinctions matter more than copying the fictional project's dates or money.
